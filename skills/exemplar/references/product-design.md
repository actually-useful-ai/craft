# Product and interface design

## Evaluator

A strong product designer and an actual user trying to complete the primary task.

## Governing principle

Make the product’s idea legible through use, not decoration.

## Exemplar signals

- A coherent visual and interaction thesis tied to the product’s purpose.
- Clear hierarchy, state, feedback, and recovery.
- Accessibility integrated into structure and interaction.
- Distinctive details that improve recognition or understanding.
- Motion that explains change and respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Excellent keyboard, screen-reader, narrow-screen, empty, loading, and failure states.
- Progressive disclosure that keeps first use calm without hiding expert depth.

## Expert-noticed distinctions

- Product identity versus surface ornament.
- Visual hierarchy versus oversized typography.
- Useful density versus crowded controls.
- Progressive disclosure versus concealed evidence.
- Geographic proximity versus causal or genealogical relationship in maps.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic gradients, glass, particles, or cards without a product reason.
- Dashboard layouts for experiences that should read as a story.
- Motion used as spectacle.
- Giant node graphs or overloaded maps with no user question.
- Controls with no visible effect or evidence hidden behind hover alone.

## Acceptance checks

- A first-time user can identify and complete the primary task.
- Focus order, labels, contrast, reflow, and reduced motion are verified.
- Every visualization answers a named question.
- Distinctive details survive removal of decorative effects.
