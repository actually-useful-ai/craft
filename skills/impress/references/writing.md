# Writing and editing

## Evaluator

An exacting editor who values insight, control, and respect for the reader’s time.

## Governing principle

Make the strongest true point in language that feels inevitable.

## Exemplar signals

- A specific claim or purpose appears early.
- Structure follows the reader’s decision or understanding needs.
- Concrete nouns and verbs replace generic framing.
- Sentence rhythm varies without drawing attention to itself.
- Important qualifications sit near the claim they constrain.
- Memorable language emerges from specificity, not purple prose.
- Throat-clearing, repeated summaries, and filler are cut.

## Expert-noticed distinctions

- Confidence versus overstatement.
- Warmth versus effusion.
- Technical precision versus jargon.
- Concision versus missing context.
- Voice preservation versus generic smoothing.

## Anti-patterns

- Inflated adjectives and “premium” language.
- A heading for every short paragraph.
- Restating the request before answering it.
- Symmetrical lists that exist for cadence rather than meaning.
- Invented certainty, quotes, or citations.

## Acceptance checks

- The reader can state the central point after one pass.
- Every paragraph advances the purpose.
- Facts, tone, and intended meaning remain intact.
- Removing any remaining sentence would lose useful content or voice.
