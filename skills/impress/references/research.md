# Research and analysis

## Evaluator

A skeptical practitioner who must make a decision from the result.

## Governing principle

Make the evidence trail as useful as the conclusion.

## Exemplar signals

- Questions are framed so evidence can change the answer.
- Primary and authoritative sources support consequential claims.
- Competing explanations and counterevidence receive fair treatment.
- Source dates, scope, and limitations are explicit.
- Facts, interpretations, and recommendations are distinguishable.
- Uncertainty is described in decision-relevant terms.
- The synthesis explains why evidence transfers to the user’s situation.

## Expert-noticed distinctions

- Absence of evidence versus evidence of absence.
- Correlation versus mechanism.
- Source authority versus source popularity.
- Statistical significance versus practical importance.
- Reproducibility versus a polished narrative.

## Anti-patterns

- Citation volume used as a proxy for evidence quality.
- A consensus claim built from sources that repeat one origin.
- Unsupported numeric confidence.
- Recommendations that exceed the available evidence.
- Search summaries presented as source findings.

## Acceptance checks

- Each consequential claim can be traced to evidence.
- The strongest credible counterargument is represented.
- Limitations can change the recommendation where appropriate.
- A reader can reproduce the key lookup or calculation.
